Research

Coolness and Distinction: A24 and American Indie Film Since 2010 — 2025 – 2026

  • Graduate thesis, Xiamen University
  • Research questions: How did A24 achieve significant economic success and cultural influence? What does this success reveal about broader changes in the indie film industry and culture? Why does a "cool" brand make sense?
  • Methodology: Following Jerome Christensen on corporate authors, A24 is treated as an author with intentions and substantial control over its products. The thesis positions A24 within the media industry and traces the relationship between its success and industrial change; draws on Bourdieu's theories of taste and distinction together with brand economics to analyze the history of cool culture and A24's brand strategy; and compares A24 with Miramax and Neon.
  • Conclusion: A24's success stems in large part from its use of emerging networked media, taste culture and brand strategy in response to a developing youth culture. In the process it has reshaped the appeal of American independent cinema among younger audiences, generating a new form of cinephilia marked by the pursuit of "cool," loyalty to the company's brand, and the affirmation of one's own identity — rather than a primary appreciation of directors' aesthetics.

Recurrent Mother–Daughter Conflicts: Archetypal Narratives, Immigrant Subjectivities and Ideological Mechanisms in Chinese American Cinema — 2024

  • Independent research project, Critical Media Theory course
  • Research question: Why has the same narrative pattern of mother–daughter conflict been reproduced by different filmmakers across different historical periods?
  • Methodology: Following Northrop Frye, the paper traces the recurring narrative back to the myth of Demeter and Persephone and reads its recurrence as a manifestation of the collective unconscious, including the "fantasy of symbiosis" proposed by psychoanalyst Iki Freud. The pattern is examined through three lenses: the immigrant experience, commerce, and ideology.
  • Conclusion: The recurring relationship reflects the breakdown of immigrants' "symbiotic fantasy" with their homeland and the identity anxieties it produces. The paper proposes that Asian American cinema has grown increasingly privatized, detaching protagonists from the broader social landscape and turning Asian American identity into a form of generic spectacle.

Chuzou (Asian Women in Departure): Female Identity and Cultural Negotiation in Wayne Wang's Films — 2022

  • Undergraduate thesis, Huaqiao University
  • Research questions: What characterizes the representation of Asian women in Wayne Wang's films, and why does it matter culturally and film-historically?
  • Methodology: Uses "chuzou" — an important topic in Chinese literary and art criticism — as the central analytical framework. Beyond physical departure, chuzou represents the power of women who resist oppression. Postcolonial perspectives are used to analyze this resilience within broader ideological and historical contexts.
  • Conclusion: Two generations of Asian American women in Wang's films depart from their homes in pursuit of their own identities and subjectivities. In contrast to the hypersexualized "Dragon Lady" stereotype of earlier American cinema, these characters open up new possibilities for representation in a globalized context.
