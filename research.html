Theses and research projects

Three studies, moving from industrial analysis of American independent film to the archetypal and ideological work of Asian American cinema. Open any title to read the questions, method and argument.

2026 Coolness and Distinction: A24 and American Indie Film Since 2010 M.A. thesis · Xiamen University

Questions How did A24 achieve both significant economic success and cultural influence? What does this success reveal about broader changes in the indie film industry and culture? Why does a "cool" brand make sense?

Method Enlightened by Jerome Christensen's idea of the corporate author, A24 is treated as an author with intentions and substantial control over its products. The thesis first positions A24 within the media industry and traces the relationship between its success and industrial change. With Bourdieu's theories of taste and distinction, together with brand economics, it then analyzes the history of cool culture and A24's brand strategy. Finally it compares A24 with Miramax and Neon.

Argument A24's success stems in large part from its use of emerging networked media, taste culture and brand strategy in response to a developing youth culture led by the new generation. In the process the company has reshaped the appeal of American independent cinema among younger audiences, generating a new form of cinephilia marked by the pursuit of "cool," loyalty to the company's brand, and the affirmation of one's own identity — rather than a primary appreciation of directors' aesthetics.

2024 Recurrent Mother–Daughter Conflicts: Archetypal Narratives, Immigrant Subjectivities and Ideological Mechanisms in Chinese American Cinema Independent research project · Critical Media Theory

Question In Chinese American cinema, why has the same narrative pattern of mother–daughter conflict been reproduced by different filmmakers across different historical periods?

Method Following Northrop Frye, the paper traces the recurring narrative back to the myth of Demeter and Persephone and reads its recurrence as a manifestation of the collective unconscious — one that includes the "fantasy of symbiosis" proposed by psychoanalyst Iki Freud. The pattern is then examined through three lenses: the immigrant experience, commerce, and ideology.

Argument The recurring mother–daughter relationship symbolically reflects the breakdown of immigrants' "symbiotic fantasy" with their homeland and the identity anxieties it produces. Driven by the pursuit of commercial success, it is also bound up with longstanding representations of Asian women in American cinema and with audiences' Orientalist imaginary. The paper proposes that Asian American cinema has grown increasingly privatized — detaching its protagonists from the broader social landscape and confining their stories to the family, a tendency that turns Asian American identity into a form of generic spectacle.

2022 Chuzou (Asian Women in Departure): Female Identity and Cultural Negotiation in Wayne Wang's Films B.A. thesis · Huaqiao University

Questions What characterizes the representation of Asian women in Wayne Wang's films, and why does it matter culturally and film-historically?

Method Uses "chuzou" — an important topic in Chinese literary and art criticism — as the central analytical framework. Beyond physical departure, chuzou represents the power of women who resist oppression. Postcolonial perspectives are then used to place this resilience within broader ideological and historical contexts.

Argument Two generations of Asian American women in Wang's films depart from their homes and established social environments in pursuit of their own identities and subjectivities. Through narratives of intergenerational conflict, ruptured intimate relationships, and reconciliation, these films represent the social dilemmas of these women and the processes of cultural negotiation. In contrast to the evil and hypersexualized "Dragon Lady" stereotype prevalent in earlier American cinema, these characters open up new possibilities for representation in a globalized context.
